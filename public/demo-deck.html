The Problem

When a KPI moves, it takes a week to know why

Speed

Root-cause diagnosis takes ~1 week. Wrong assumptions drive decisions for 7 days before anyone gets an answer.

Coverage

Analysts answer the stated question, not the real one. Quest-completing users had no next step — teams were looking for churn signals instead.

Actionability

Even when the right cause is found, there's no playbook. The insight lands in Slack and dies there.

The goal: Surface cohort-level insights before anyone asks — automatically identifying why KPIs move, which customer segments need intervention, and what to do next. Root-cause time: 1 week → same day.

What the BigQuery SQL actually does

Login Definition

First session on iOS, Android, or Web after the purchase date. Sources: screen_viewed page_viewed identifies auth0_logs — all unified with UNION ALL.

Denominator: New/Trial, first orders only (exclude renewals & upgrades)

Activation Definition

First real content play after purchase. Counts:

  • Quest asset played (not preview_mode)
  • Meditation media played
  • Eve AI prompted (not onboarding)
  • Shorts played
  • Channel content played

Day 0 Decline — What We Found

We detected a structural drop in Day 0 login after June 29, 2026.

The app runs a counterfactual decomposition:

  • Mix shift impact — did the product mix change to lower-performing products?
  • Rate change impact — did individual product login rates fall within-product?

This tells us whether to fix the marketing mix or fix the product onboarding flow.

How Refunds Are Measured

  • Source: fact_sales_order.refund_timestamp — the actual refund event, not the cancellation
  • Window: 15 days from purchase date (consistent with BQ definition)
  • Denominator excludes Trial and Not Applicable — they can't be charged so can't be refunded
  • Cohort must be 22+ days old to be marked mature (refund window complete)

The Collaboration Model

What Sarah brought vs what Claude brought

Sarah — Domain Intelligence
  • Knew the real Mindvalley BigQuery table names and schemas
  • Defined what Login and Activation mean in the product context
  • Called out when the data looked wrong (e.g. refund_timestamp vs cancel as refund proxy)
  • Decided which segments matter to the business (MC funnel, funnel quest, price bands)
  • Reviewed each sprint against acceptance criteria
  • Caught edge cases: Trial orders can't be refunded, in-app buyers have no device signal
Claude — Execution Speed
  • Wrote the 800-line BigQuery datacube SQL from the table definitions Sarah provided
  • Built the full Next.js app, SQLite schema, API routes, and UI components
  • Translated metric definitions into correct SQL (SAFE_DIVIDE, NULLIF, mature cohort cutoffs)
  • Implemented counterfactual decomposition (mix shift vs rate change analysis)
  • Iterated rapidly when reviews failed — retry cycle within the same session
  • Maintained schema version tracking so DB auto-rebuilds on structure changes

The pattern: Sarah drove the what and why — the business definitions, the data contracts, the product decisions. Claude drove the how — SQL, TypeScript, architecture, iteration. Neither could have produced this alone at this speed.
